Threading my trading engine didn't make it faster

September 2026

Pulse is a market data and execution engine I've been writing in C++20. A binary feed comes in, the engine rebuilds order books, and strategies send orders through risk checks to a simulated exchange. The obvious next step was to split that pipeline across threads: one for the feed, one for the books, one for strategy and risk, connected by lock-free queues. More cores, more throughput. That was the plan.

It didn't work out that way, and figuring out why taught me more than the parts that did work.

The numbers

Same workload (8 instruments, market maker running), single thread versus three threads, measured on an M1 Pro:

pipeline                 batch   throughput
inline (1 thread)          -     11.04 M events/s
mutex + condvar            1      2.57 M/s
mutex + condvar           64      4.17 M/s
SPSC ring buffer           1      4.30 M/s
SPSC ring buffer          64     11.29 M/s

Handing events over one at a time, the threaded version was less than half as fast as a single thread. With batches of 64 it only just caught up.

Why

Each event takes about 85 ns of real work. At that size, moving it between cores is not free. The book update I pass from the book thread to the core thread is 192 bytes, which is three cache lines, and every one of them has to travel from one core's cache to another's. Do that per event and the cost of the hand-off is comparable to the work itself.

Batching amortizes the queue overhead, which is how it gets back to parity. But the pipeline is only as fast as its slowest stage, and the book stage alone takes about 40 ns per event. Three threads can't go faster than that.

Where the queue design mattered

The queue itself was the biggest surprise. My first lock-free ring buffer was slower than a plain mutex queue. The producer's write index and the consumer's read index sat on the same cache line, so every push and pop fought over it. Padding them onto separate cache lines made the queue 2 to 3 times faster, and caching the other side's index added another 1.5x. Lock-free doesn't help much if the two threads still share a cache line.

What threading did buy

Throughput isn't everything. At a steady 1M messages per second, the SPSC pipeline's median latency was 0.92 µs, while the mutex version's was 33.5 µs. The mutex consumer goes to sleep when the queue is empty, and each wake-up is a trip through the kernel. Threading also isolates the feed: the feed thread keeps draining the socket even when strategy code is busy, and recording and telemetry never touch the hot path.

So the lesson was: measure the per-item work before splitting it up. If the work is small, the hand-off dominates. The next step for Pulse is sharding order books across threads by instrument, so each thread does a whole event's worth of work instead of a slice of every event.
